# Office move: Friday 6pm to Monday 8am

For 12 people, the weekend itself is the easy part. A mover can load and unload an office this size in one day. What decides whether people can work at 8am Monday is mostly done in the weeks before. The two most important items are working internet at the new building and permission to use that building over the weekend. Internet means a live, tested line, not an order placed. Weekend use means after-hours access, a booked lift or loading dock, and the insurance certificate the landlord will want from the mover. A business internet line can take several weeks to install. Order it before anything else. If it isn't live by the Thursday before, the move goes ahead with a fallback (below) rather than everyone arriving Monday to no network.

I don't know the move date, what equipment you have (servers, desk phones, printers), or the state of the new space. I've written dates relative to the move weekend: "M-4w" means four weeks before move Friday.

## Questions I would ask

1. **Which weekend is it, and is it fixed?** Default: at least six weeks away. If it's less than four weeks away, place the internet order today, plan the 4G/5G fallback as the main option for the first week, and book whichever mover can take the date.
2. **Is internet already installed at the new building, and who provides it?** Default: no, it needs ordering. This is the item with the longest lead time.
3. **Is there anything on site beyond laptops: a server or NAS, desk phones, a door-access or alarm system, a large printer?** Default: laptops plus a shared printer and Wi-Fi equipment, with files in the cloud. If there's an on-site server, it gets its own shutdown, backup and restart steps on Friday night, and a backup restore test beforehand.
4. **Can we still get into the old office after Monday?** Default: the keys go back a few days later, which allows a second trip for anything left behind. If the lease ends Friday at midnight, everything has to be out Friday night and the old office needs its own cleaning and handover slot.
5. **Who runs this, and who handles IT?** Default: one office manager in charge plus one named backup, and IT done by an outside provider booked for Saturday. Budget is not set here. You give the total, and the main costs are the mover, the IT provider's weekend time, and any new furniture.

Answer first: 1 and 2. Together they tell us whether the internet line can be ready in time, and that decides the rest.

## Plan

**From M-6w (or now): commitments**
- Order internet for the new building. Ask for an install date at least a week before the move, and a test of the live line.
- Get the landlord's rules for moving in writing, for both buildings: weekend access hours, lift or dock booking, protection for floors and walls, the mover's insurance certificate, and where a truck can park. Book the lift or dock for Saturday.
- Get two or three quotes from movers who handle offices. Confirm they can supply the insurance certificate, crates and labels. Book one for Saturday, with the crates delivered the week before.
- Book the IT provider (or name the in-house person) for Friday evening and Saturday, plus a 7:30am check on Monday.

**M-4w: inventory and layout**
- Draw a floor plan of the new space with a numbered seat for each person, plus printer, network cabinet and shared areas. Every crate and piece of furniture is labelled with the number of where it goes.
- Decide what isn't moving: old furniture, paper archives, broken equipment. Book disposal or donation for the week before, not the move weekend.
- Make a list of address changes: post forwarding, bank, suppliers, website, Google/Maps listing, invoices and letterhead, insurance. Make most of them in the week of the move, but set up post forwarding now.

**M-1w: confirm and pack**
- Internet live and tested at the new site: speed test, Wi-Fi access points mounted, printer reachable.
- Tell the team the arrangements: pack your own desk by Friday 5pm into labelled crates, take laptops home, and where to go Monday. Also send the fallback rule (below), so nobody hears about it for the first time on Sunday night.
- Send the new address and move date to clients and suppliers once the mover and lift booking are confirmed.

**Thursday before: go/no-go.** All of these must be true to go ahead as planned:
- Internet at the new site has been live and tested for at least one working day.
- The mover has confirmed in writing the Saturday time, the truck size and the number of crew.
- The lift or dock booking and weekend access cards/keys for both buildings are in hand.
- The insurance certificate has been accepted by both landlords.
- Any server has a fresh backup that has been restored successfully.

If the internet item fails and everything else passes, move anyway and use the fallback. If the mover, access or insurance items fail, move the date. A move without access or insurance can be stopped at the door on Saturday morning.

**Friday 6pm to 10pm: shut down and prepare**
- The IT person shuts down and labels the network equipment, printer and any server. Photograph the cabling before it comes apart.
- The office manager walks through and checks that every crate and item has a destination number. Anything unlabelled stays.
- Valuables and sensitive items (cash, cheque books, signed contracts, server disks) go in a car with a named person, not the truck.

**Saturday: move**
- Morning: load at the old office, unload at the new one, and place everything by its number. The office manager stays at the new site to direct. The backup person is at the old site until the truck leaves empty.
- Afternoon: the IT person sets up the network cabinet, printer and server, and tests every seat's port or Wi-Fi.
- Abort rule: if the truck hasn't left the old building by 3pm (for example, the lift isn't available), stop loading. Move the essentials first: network equipment, printer, and one crate per person. Schedule the rest for Sunday.

**Sunday: buffer and check**
- Kept free on purpose, for anything Saturday didn't finish.
- By 4pm, someone sits at three different desks and checks login, internet, printing and a video call. If that fails and can't be fixed by 6pm, send the fallback message.

**Monday 7:30am**
- The IT person and the office manager are on site before anyone else arrives. Expect small requests (monitor cables, chairs) for the first two days.
- Second trip to the old office later in the week for anything left behind. Final walk-through and key return, with photos for the deposit.

**Fallback rule (sent in advance, in M-1w):** If the new office isn't ready Sunday 6pm, everyone works from home on Monday, and the office manager confirms the new date by message. If the internet line is the only thing missing, the office opens on a 4G/5G router as a temporary connection. Book one in M-1w if the line isn't confirmed by then.

## Found, not touched
- Physical security at the new site: alarm codes, door access lists and who holds keys have to be set up before Monday. The plan assumes the landlord handles this.
- Health and safety: fire exits, first aid kit and fire-warden roles at the new building. This is usually a short briefing in the first week.

## Need from you
- The move date and the old office's lease end date.
- The new building's floor plan and the landlord's moving rules.
- A list of equipment beyond laptops, and who handles IT today.
