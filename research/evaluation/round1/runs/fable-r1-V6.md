# Office move plan: 12 people, Friday 18:00 to Monday 08:00

The weekend is enough time for an office this size; the physical move takes about one day. What makes weekend moves fail is almost always something with a lead time longer than a weekend: the internet line at the new building, building access for the movers, and insurance paperwork. So most of this plan happens before Friday.

**Definition of done:** on Monday at 08:00, all 12 people can sit down, log in, get online, print and take calls, and the old office is empty.

## Go/no-go conditions

Check these on the Wednesday before. If the first one is not true, postpone or arrange the fallback now.

1. **Internet at the new building is live and has been tested on site**, not merely scheduled. Fallback: a 5G/LTE business router, bought and tested in advance, plus a work-from-home Monday.
2. Keys or access cards for the new building are in hand, and the lease allows access from Friday evening.
3. Both buildings have confirmed weekend access in writing: loading dock, freight lift booking, parking for the truck, alarm codes, heating and lighting outside office hours.
4. The movers are confirmed in writing with arrival time, crew size and insurance certificate. Many buildings require the certificate before the crew may enter.

## Before the move

**4 to 6 weeks out**
- Order internet for the new building. It has the longest lead time of anything on the list. Ask for installation at least a week before the move.
- Book movers: get 2 or 3 quotes, each after a walkthrough of both sites. Ask for crates, IT packing, and furniture disassembly and reassembly to be included.
- Name one move lead with authority to decide on the day, and one IT lead (internal, or your IT provider booked for Saturday and Monday morning).
- Draw the floor plan of the new office with a number for every desk and a name for every room. Assign each person a desk number.
- Walk the new space: count power sockets and network ports per desk, check Wi-Fi coverage, and measure doors and the lift against the largest items (conference table, safe, large printer).
- Decide what does not move: old furniture, archives, dead equipment. Arrange disposal, donation or shredding.

**2 to 3 weeks out**
- Notify staff, clients, suppliers, bank, insurer, tax office and company registry, post office (mail forwarding), couriers, payroll and benefits providers, and software vendors that hold a billing address.
- Phones: confirm that numbers are being ported, or that the VoIP system only needs plugging in.
- Order crates, labels in one colour per room, monitor and PC bags, cable ties, and floor and wall protection if the movers do not supply them.
- Arrange cleaning for the new office (Friday, before arrival) and the old one (Sunday or Monday).
- Order keys, access cards and alarm codes for 12 people.
- Prepare the address change on the website, Google Business Profile, e-mail signatures, invoice templates and letterhead, all timed for Monday.

**Move week**
- Monday: send everyone the packing instructions, their desk number and the plan for the following Monday.
- Wednesday: go/no-go check. The IT lead verifies backups by restoring something, and photographs the back of the server, the network rack and any complex desk setup.
- Thursday: non-essential items, archives and storage rooms packed.
- Friday by 16:00: everyone packs their own desk. Each crate, monitor, PC, chair and peripheral gets a label with name and desk number. Laptops and personal valuables go home with their owners. The fridge is emptied.
- Friday 17:00: the IT lead shuts down servers and network equipment in order.

## The weekend

**Friday**
- 18:00 Movers arrive. The move lead walks them through, pointing out fragile items and what stays.
- 18:00 to 21:00 Disconnect IT, pack shared areas, load. For a 12-person office the truck can often be loaded on Friday night. Whether it is unloaded the same night or on Saturday morning depends on the mover and on the new building's rules.
- IT equipment travels with the IT lead, or in a separately marked load that goes on last and comes off first.

**Saturday**
- 08:00 Unload. One person stands at the entrance of the new office with the floor plan and directs every item to its numbered desk or named room.
- Morning: furniture placed and assembled.
- From about 10:00, in parallel, the IT lead brings up the network in this order: internet line, firewall or router, switches, Wi-Fi, server or NAS, printers, phones. The network comes before the desks, because nothing at a desk can be tested without it.
- Afternoon: desks set up (dock, monitors, keyboard, phone). Every desk is tested: power, network or Wi-Fi, a login, a print job. The meeting-room screen and conferencing are tested with a real call.
- End of day: a list of what is not working.

**Sunday (buffer and finish)**
- Morning: work through Saturday's list. This day exists so that a problem found on Saturday still has 24 hours.
- Empty crates stacked for collection, packaging removed, signage up, kitchen and supplies stocked, first-aid kit and fire extinguishers in place, emergency exits clear.
- Old office: final walkthrough with photos, meter readings, cleaning, anything left behind collected. Keys returned as the lease requires.
- 16:00 The move lead and IT lead walk the new office desk by desk against the checklist.
- 18:00 Message to all staff: confirmation that Monday is on, the address, how to get in, where to park, and who to ask for help.

**Monday**
- 07:00 Move lead and IT lead on site. The IT lead has no meetings until noon.
- 08:00 Staff arrive, receive keys or cards, and unpack their own crate.
- 08:30 Ten-minute walk-round: exits, alarm, kitchen, toilets, printer, Wi-Fi name.
- Snags go on a whiteboard or shared document, with triage at noon and at 16:00.
- During the week: crates collected, old office handed over, remaining address changes completed.

## Who does what

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Move lead | Plan, movers, both buildings, decisions on the day, single phone number for everyone |
| IT lead | Internet, shutdown and startup, desk testing, Monday support |
| Each employee | Packs and labels own desk on Friday, takes laptop home, unpacks on Monday |
| 2 or 3 volunteers | Saturday: directing items and setting up desks. Agree pay or time off in lieu beforehand |
| Movers | Crates, loading, transport, furniture, placing by label |

## Main risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Internet not live | Order early, test on site before the move, 5G router as backup, work-from-home Monday as the last resort |
| Lift or dock unavailable, or the building refuses the movers | Written booking with both building managers, insurance certificate sent ahead |
| Something does not fit through a door or lift | Measure in advance; disassembly included in the mover's quote |
| Data loss or a damaged server | Verified backup before shutdown; the IT lead transports the drives personally |
| Items lost or on the wrong desk | Everything labelled with a desk number; one person directing at the door |
| Movers late or absent | Written confirmation on Thursday, a second company's number at hand, Sunday as buffer |
| Monday chaos | Staff unpack only their own crate, the IT lead is free all morning, snags go on the list |

## Budget lines to expect

Movers and crates; internet installation and possibly a month of overlap at both sites; the IT provider's weekend hours; cleaning of both offices; disposal; keys, cards and signage; small cabling and power strips; food for the weekend crew; a contingency of 10 to 15 percent.

## Questions I would ask

1. **How far ahead is the move?** Default: at least 4 weeks. Under 2 weeks, internet becomes the critical item and I would plan on the 5G fallback from the start.
2. **How far apart are the buildings, and on which floors?** Default: same city, under 30 minutes apart, both with a lift.
3. **Is furniture moving, or is the new office furnished?** Default: desks, chairs and storage all move. If the new office is furnished, the move fits into Saturday alone.
4. **What IT is on site?** Default: laptops with docks and monitors, one small server or NAS, one network rack, one or two printers, cloud e-mail and files, VoIP phones. A server room or a traditional phone system would add work and need an earlier start.
5. **Is there an IT person or provider?** Default: an external provider who can be booked for Saturday and Monday morning.
6. **Is there a budget for professional movers?** Default: yes. I would not have staff carry furniture, because of injury liability and insurance.
7. **When do the leases start and end?** Default: a few days of overlap, so the new office can be cleaned and the internet tested beforehand, and the old one handed over the following week.
8. **Can everyone work from home on Monday if needed?** Default: yes, as a fallback only.
9. **Does anything have special requirements?** Default: no. Examples that would change the plan: a safe, confidential paper archives, lab or medical equipment, a large plotter, regulated data.
10. **Does the new space need any fit-out (cabling, partitions, painting)?** Default: it is ready to occupy, with working power and network ports.
