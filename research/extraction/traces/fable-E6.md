# Two-day offsite for 40 people, three months out

Three months is enough time, but only if the venue and dates are fixed in the first two weeks. Everything else can be adjusted later; a venue that sleeps and seats 40 cannot be found at short notice.

Below is a plan worked back from the event, built on the defaults in the next section. The first four questions change the plan the most, so answer those first if you can.

## Questions I would ask

| # | Question | Default I am assuming |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the offsite for: strategy, team bonding, a planning cycle, a reward? | A mix: about half working sessions, half time together. |
| 2 | What is the total budget, or the per-person figure? | Not set. I give a percentage split below; you supply the total. |
| 3 | Where are the 40 people based: one city, or spread out? | Mostly one city, with a few who travel in. |
| 4 | Are the dates fixed or flexible? | Flexible within a two-week window around mid-February 2027; midweek (Tuesday to Wednesday or Wednesday to Thursday). |
| 5 | Overnight stay, or two day-trips? | One night away, within about two hours of the office by coach or train. |
| 6 | Who decides, and who does the work? | One executive sponsor who approves budget and agenda; one organiser with a helper. |
| 7 | Is attendance expected of everyone? | Yes, during paid working days. |
| 8 | Any constraints: alcohol policy, travel policy, people who cannot stay overnight? | Standard company policy; some people will need accommodation for caring duties, diet or access. |

## Timeline

### Weeks 1 to 2: Lock the things that cannot move

- Agree purpose, budget ceiling and decision-maker with the sponsor.
- Pick two or three candidate date pairs. Check them against public holidays, school holidays, company deadlines and key people's leave.
- Send a one-question poll to all 40: which dates are impossible for you.
- Brief three to five venues: 40 people, one plenary room, three or four breakout spaces, about 40 single rooms, meals, AV and step-free access. Ask each for a written quote and its cancellation terms.
- **Sign the venue and room block by the end of week 2.** This is the one deadline that matters.
- Send a save-the-date the day the contract is signed.

### Weeks 3 to 5: Shape the event

- Draft the agenda with the sponsor (skeleton below). Decide what the offsite must produce: decisions, a plan, or simply better working relationships.
- Book transport: coach from the office, or train block booking. Book flights for anyone coming from elsewhere.
- Decide whether to use an outside facilitator. If yes, book now.
- Choose and book one group activity and the main dinner.
- Send the registration form: dietary needs, access needs, room needs, travel origin, emergency contact, anything that would stop them taking part in the activity.

### Weeks 6 to 9: Fill in the detail

- Confirm session owners and what each will prepare.
- Send final numbers and dietary list to the venue and caterer.
- Plan breakout groups ahead of time, mixing teams.
- Order materials: name badges, flip charts, markers, sticky notes, any printed material.
- Arrange travel insurance or confirm company cover.
- Send the full attendee briefing at week 8: schedule, travel, what to bring, dress, expenses rules.

### Weeks 10 to 12: Final checks

- Walk through the venue, in person if it is close, or by video call. Test projector, sound, wifi and room layout.
- Confirm every booking in writing: venue, transport, dinner, activity, facilitator.
- Print the run sheet with timings, owners and phone numbers.
- Send a reminder three days before, with the meeting point and time.
- Name a second person who can run the event if the organiser is ill.

### Week after

- Send a five-question survey within two days.
- Circulate decisions and action owners within a week. An offsite with no follow-up is the most common complaint afterwards.
- Reconcile the budget.

## Agenda skeleton

| | Day 1 | Day 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Travel, arrive by 10:30. Opening from the sponsor: why we are here, what we will leave with. First working session. | Working session 3: turn day 1 into decisions and owners. |
| Midday | Lunch. | Lunch. |
| Afternoon | Working session 2 in breakouts. Group activity from about 16:00. | Short closing session: commitments, next steps. Leave by 15:30. |
| Evening | Dinner. Nothing compulsory afterwards. | Home at a reasonable hour. |

Guidelines: sessions of 90 minutes at most, with real breaks; more small-group time than presentations; unstructured time is part of the purpose, so do not fill it.

## Budget split

Use this to check quotes until a total is set.

| Item | Share of budget |
|---|---|
| Accommodation | 30 to 35% |
| Venue hire, meeting rooms, AV | 15 to 20% |
| Food and drink | 20 to 25% |
| Transport | 10 to 15% |
| Activity and facilitator | 5 to 10% |
| Contingency | 10% |

Many venues offer a 24-hour delegate rate that bundles room, meeting space and meals. It is usually cheaper and simpler than pricing each separately.

## Roles

- **Sponsor:** owns purpose, opens and closes the event, approves spend.
- **Organiser:** owns the plan, contracts and run sheet.
- **Helper:** registration, travel, attendee questions.
- **Session owners:** one named person per session.
- **On-site lead:** one person with every supplier's phone number and authority to make decisions on the day.

## What tends to go wrong

| Problem | Preparation |
|---|---|
| Transport delay or bad weather | Keep the first morning slot light; have a coach company number; know the venue's weather cancellation terms. |
| Drop-outs or late additions | Ask the venue for the date until which numbers can change without charge; put it in the calendar. |
| AV failure | Bring adapters, a spare laptop and a printed copy of key slides. |
| Dietary or access needs missed | Collect in week 3 to 5, confirm with the venue in writing, re-check on arrival. |
| Someone falls ill or is hurt | Emergency contacts on the run sheet; know the nearest pharmacy and hospital; one first-aider identified. |
| The agenda overruns | A timekeeper per session; decide in advance which session is cut first. |
